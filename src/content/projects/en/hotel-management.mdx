## Overview

> **Unconfirmed draft.** I wrote the two decisions below from the repository description, before you
> gave me the facts. Confirm, correct or replace them.

A group of guesthouses ran their bookings on shared spreadsheets. Every property had its own, each
with its own pricing rules, and nobody could answer a simple question: how many rooms are free next
week, across all of them?

I built the whole platform, a Laravel API and a Next.js dashboard. One requirement shaped the
architecture: it had to be multi-tenant. Each property needed its own data, users and rates without
turning into six separate installations to maintain.

## Technical decisions

### One database, tenant isolation at the query layer

Instead of a database per property, I used a single schema with `tenant_id` on every table and a
global Eloquent scope that never lets a query escape the current tenant.

> **What it cost:** a bug in that scope leaks data between clients, a risk separate databases would
> not carry. I took it because running migrations across six databases on every release would have
> been the everyday problem, and leakage is the kind of problem tests can cover.

### Availability computed in the database

Searching for free rooms became a date-range query in PostgreSQL rather than loading every booking
and cross-checking it in PHP.

> **What it cost:** the most important business rule in the system lives in SQL, out of reach of
> unit tests. In exchange, the search answers in constant time as the group grows.
